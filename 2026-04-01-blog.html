Philippians 1:6 (NIV)

Testimony

Max grew up without church or spiritual grounding, shaped by an unstable military family life. Constant moves and emotional distance taught him to blend in, masking wounds behind whatever identity fit the moment. His sense of right and wrong shifted like sand.

When he reached Panama City as a teenager, freedom turned to bondage. His parents’ divorce at sixteen drove him into despair, believing love was temporary and life meaningless. Drugs, crime, and near-death experiences followed. Yet even in the chaos, a whisper cut through: “What are you doing? I made you for more.”

One Easter, dope-sick and desperate, Max stumbled upon a film about Jesus. For the first time, the Gospel felt real—Jesus had died for him. On May 4, he boarded a bus to Evans Training Center with nothing but a pocket Bible and a flicker of faith. Detox was brutal, but truth, confession, and Scripture began to rebuild his heart. Sanctification came slowly, not through perfection but persistence.

Today Max is a father, worship leader, and servant in ministry. His story radiates mercy, peace, and the steady joy of redemption. He refuses shallow religion; instead, he lives out a faith that keeps pressing forward, trusting that even when life doesn’t work out, God is still working within.

"Even if it doesn’t work out, God is working it out of you." — Max, Sojourner Collective

Reflection

Maybe you’ve believed the lie that you’ve gone too far. Max’s story reminds us that God’s arm is never too short to save. No addiction, no past, no failure can silence His call: “I made you for more.”
